# Carousel 02 — The Silent Quarter

デッキ: `carousel-02-silent-quarter.html` → 同名 `.pdf`（12ページ）
形式: LinkedIn ドキュメント投稿
狙うICP: SaaS / B2B。ただし共感の射程が最も広く、全ICP横断で反応する

## 投稿本文（そのまま貼る）

```
Three weeks of silence from a Japanese company is not rejection.

It's usually the opposite.

In most Western companies one senior person says yes and the organisation
follows. In many Japanese companies the sequence runs the other way:
agreement is assembled first, and the signature is the last step rather
than the first.

Which means the person you're emailing may genuinely want to buy — and may
have no authority to say so yet.

I mapped the five stages a Japanese buying decision actually moves through.
The uncomfortable part is how few of them you are in:

1. Quiet collection — you're visible
2. Internal translation — you're absent
3. Pre-consensus — you're absent
4. Formal approval — you're absent
5. The order — suddenly urgent

You are present for stages 1 and 5. Everything that decides the outcome
happens in 2, 3 and 4 — carried through a building by someone you have
never met, using only what you handed over before it went quiet.

That reframes the job. You are not selling a product. You are supplying
raw material for an internal document you will never see.

The 12 pages below cover each stage, what to send at each one, the three
moves that quietly kill your internal advocate, and how to tell when
silence really is a no.

Have you had a Japanese deal go quiet? How long did you wait before you
wrote it off?
```

## 内容の要点

- 中核主張: 決定は発表される前に製造されている。西洋型の「説得の瞬間」が構造的に存在しない
- 5段階のうち売り手が居るのは1と5のみ。8ページ目でPresent/Absentを視覚化している
- 最も強い再定義: 「商品を売っているのではなく、二度と見ることのない社内文書の原料を供給している」
- 10ページ目「社内の推薦者を殺す3手」= 期限設定・値引き・上長へのエスカレーション。いずれも他市場では標準手法
- 11ページ目で「本当に終わっている3つの兆候」を提示し、無限に待てとは言わない

## 注意

法令・税制への言及はない。組織行動の一般論であり、断定を避けた記述にしてある。
