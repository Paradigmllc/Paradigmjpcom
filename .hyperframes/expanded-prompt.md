# Pet Life Movie production breakdown

## Style

Use the exact palette and typography in `design.md`: warm charcoal `#17131A`, ivory `#F7F1E8`, ink `#2B202A`, coral `#D97A62`, ochre `#E8A838`, violet `#8D7BAF`, sage `#71856F`; BIZ UDPMincho/Fraunces for emotional copy and IBM Plex Mono for metadata. The result must feel like a small editorial film authored from real family material.

## Rhythm

`opening-breathe-build-PEAK-resolve`

## Global direction

- Preserve source-photo identity. Paid hero shots may use source-conditioned GPU micro-motion only; reject identity drift and fall back to the supplied photo. Never create a new pet or family member.
- Maintain three depth layers: blurred photo atmosphere, primary photo, foreground caption/film details.
- Use deterministic Ken Burns, lateral pan, perspective drift, and focus-pull motion, rotating the treatment scene by scene.
- Use one primary transition per template and one restrained accent transition at the emotional peak.
- Captions remain inside orientation-aware title-safe and social-control-safe regions.
- Every decorative element has a timeline-bound drift, breath, or grain movement.

## Beat 1 — The first page

Concept: The film opens as if a cherished album has just been found. The first photograph is already present behind a soft veil; the pet's name becomes the invitation into the memory.

Mood: Intimate editorial title sequence, tactile paper and photographic warmth.

Depth: BG blurred duplicate and vignette; MG source photo; FG pet name, fine rule, date-like scene counter, grain.

Choreography: Background BREATHES, image FOCUSES and PUSHES, name DRAWS on, rule FILLS, scene marker TYPES on.

Transition: template primary transition, 0.38–0.8 s according to template energy.

## Beats 2–4 — Life in motion

Concept: Each photograph reveals a different texture of daily life. Movement follows the implied direction in the image instead of applying a repeated zoom.

Mood: Warm documentary with moments of playful scrapbook energy.

Depth: BG accent wash and blurred photo; MG framed source photo; FG factual caption, scene index, subtle registration marks, hairline progress.

Choreography: Photos PAN, DRIFT, or SETTLE; captions SLIDE or WRITE in with varied timing; small marks PULSE once and hold. At least two focal points per beat.

Transition: primary focus pull/page reveal; accent light leak or paper wipe only at the strongest memory.

## Beat 5 — Emotional peak

Concept: The most personal factual memory receives the most breathing room. The image fills the frame while typography becomes quieter and more confident.

Mood: Cinematic, sincere, never melodramatic.

Depth: BG slow accent bloom; MG full-bleed photo; FG short caption, fine frame, restrained metadata.

Choreography: Bloom EXPANDS, photo PUSHES slowly, caption SETTLES, frame DRAWS. Hold for reading.

Transition: strongest template accent transition, then decelerate into the close.

## Final beat — Always family

Concept: Resolve on the pet's name and the factual closing line. This is a keepsake ending, not a sales CTA.

Mood: Quiet certainty and closure.

Depth: BG final photo with vignette; MG soft ivory title plate or dark-canvas title depending on template; FG name, closing line, tiny Pet Life Movie provenance mark.

Choreography: Final image FOCUSES, name RISES, closing line FADES in, grain BREATHES, then the whole scene DIPS gently to the canvas.

## Recurring motifs

- A hairline progress rule grows across scenes.
- Scene index uses IBM Plex Mono and tabular numerals.
- A single accent color connects caption plate, registration mark, and transition wash.

## Negative prompt

No invented animals, face morphing, changed fur markings, fake camera shake, neon gradients, tiny web typography, captions beneath social controls, identical crossfades, abrupt blank frames, wall-clock animation, random motion, customer notification from QA mode, or automated final approval.
